# Folio first-run walkthrough and change plan

Nov 17, 2026. Branch `samantha/v2-research` (the version now on Sams-working-branch).

## How I ran it

- I wiped the browser (saved data, session, offline cache) so every surface opened as if it was the first time.
- Phone surfaces were tested at 375 by 812. The portal and adult view were tested at 1280 by 800.
- Each walkthrough starts at "I just got Folio" and stops at that person's main home dashboard.
- **Personas come from your files.** Real participants are named with their code (P1, P2 paediatrician, P3 family doctor, P4, P6, C1 and C2 classmates). The AI personas (Riley, Jamie, Sasha, Dr. Taylor, Casey) are hypotheses only and are labelled that way.
- **Outside research** came from three background searches: teens, young adults and parents, then clinicians and program leads, then your own transcripts. The sources are listed at the end.
- **Gaps in your interviews.** We interviewed no teen under 18, no parent, no nurse educator, no adult endocrinologist and no program lead. Where I speak for them, I lean on published research and say so.

## The one-line finding

**Folio has no first run.** On a new phone, the splash goes straight to "Evening, Maya" with another teen's plant, notes and card. The portal opens already signed in as Dr. Okafor. Only the adult view has a real first step, its consent screen. Every persona below hits this before anything else.

## 1. Eli, 13, just enrolled at his clinic

*Basis: seed patient Eli T. (level 1, no card yet). Riley (AI, 14) is a hypothesis only. P2 says transition prep usually starts "around age 15 or 16".*

**Screen 1. The link from the clinic opens in Safari.**
- I expect: a page that tells me what this is and asks me to sign in or set up.
- I see: the Folio splash (cards, red star, "Your story, carried forward") for about 2.5 seconds.
- I think: "Nice, but carried forward to where? I still don't know what this does."

**Screen 2. Home.**
- I expect: "Hi Eli, let's set you up."
- I see: "Evening, Maya", her plant, her level, a note from her doctor and her card with her ID.
- I think: "Is this the wrong app? Am I looking at someone else's health stuff?" This is the worst moment in the whole product. It breaks trust and shows one teen's details to another.
- To become me, I'd need to find About me, scroll to the bottom, open Prototype and pick my name from a list. No real teen would find that.

**Screen 3. Home as Eli (once switched).**
- I see: "Family does everything. 0 of 5 skills on your own." Then five skills, every one marked Learning.
- I think: "So I'm bad at everything." The words describe what I can't do, not where to start.
- I see a card with my name and "Tap to share", but the clinic hasn't given me a card yet. It promises something I don't have.
- The plant is a tiny seed in a pot. "Practise a skill, read a guide or tick off a care task to grow a leaf." Which one? There's no button that does any of it from here.

**Reached home: yes, but only by a hidden switch, and the first thing I learned is that I'm behind.**

## 2. Maya, 17, near transfer

*Basis: sample patient. No real 17 year old was interviewed. P4 looked back on this age and C2 read Maya's profile.*

- **Splash, then Home.** It's my own data, so it works. But on a new phone I'd still expect to prove it's me before my notes appear.
- **Plant card.** "Young plant. Stage 4 of 10 · Level 4, Contributor." Two counting systems for one thing. "Family guides, I contribute" reads like a slogan, not a sentence.
- **Note from Dr. Okafor.** It says "For the adult team: Maya does best when the plan is clear" and underneath "Shown to future care teams". That note is actually a **draft**, and drafts are hidden from adult teams, so the label is wrong. It's also a note about me written for someone else, sitting where I expect a message to me. The note that is to me ("Practise your low-sugar plan out loud") is lower in the list.
- **What I'd want first.** P4: "100% of the responsibility fell on me" for booking and refills. The Run my care tasks I need are two taps away on Levels.

**Reached home: yes. The level wording and the note slot are the two things I'd fix.**

## 3. P4, 19, already in adult care

*Basis: P4 (real). She suggested the plant herself.*

- She wants to learn, not log: "let's teach you how to manage it" (P4). Her pump already logs.
- She wants one card that works everywhere: "I can just tap in and boom all of my history is there" (P4).
- **First open:** the same "Evening, Maya" problem. There's no path for someone who has already moved. A transferred patient's Home says "You have moved to adult care", but she'd never reach it without the Prototype switch.
- Asked whether her 15-year-old self would have cared about levels: "Do you want me to be honest... No" (P4). The plant was her idea because it "would incentivize me rather than just being level".

**Reached home: only through the hidden switch. When she gets there, the Home leads with the level, not the learning she asked for.**

## 4. Priya, Maya's mother

*Basis: no parent was interviewed. Second-hand from P4, P1, P2, P3 and P6. Jamie (AI) is a hypothesis only.*

- She hears about Folio at clinic and opens the same link.
- She expects a parent sign-in, or at least an explanation of her role.
- She sees Maya's teen app and can edit everything in it. There's no parent view at all.
- What the research says she needs: a role that moves from doing to backing up, guidance on how to talk about it, and to see skills and sign-offs, never glucose numbers or private notes (Fremont and Miller 2021, Goethals 2019, Pritlove 2020).
- P3: "parents feel like they've dropped off the face of the earth." P4's mom still "does all that still low-key" (supplies, refills).

**Reached home: there is no parent home to reach.**

## 5. Nina Park, diabetes nurse educator, first day on the portal

*Basis: Nina is on the seed team. Sasha (AI) is a hypothesis only. P1, P2, P3 and P6 are real.*

**Screen 1. Portal link.**
- I expect: my hospital sign-in, then a short "here's how this fits your clinic".
- I see: a 2.4 second splash, then I'm already "Dr. A. Okafor, Admin". There's no sign-in, and nothing says Nina.

**Screen 2. Home.**
- I see: 7 enrolled, 2 need attention, 2 ready to advance, "Average level 4.4 of 10", a stair chart and recent activity.
- I think: clear and calm. But "Average level" is a program number, not something I act on during clinic.
- What I'm worried about, in real participants' words: "More paperwork, oh my god" (P3). "this isn't even anything extra because they're already doing the assessments" (P1) is the message I need to hear first, and nothing on screen says it.
- Nothing tells me what I'm allowed to do as a nurse. The Team page says nurses can't sign off a level, but I'd never know that from Home.

**Reached home: instantly, but as the wrong person and with no sense of how Folio fits a visit.**

## 6. Casey, program lead (and P6, quality specialist)

*Basis: Casey (AI) is a hypothesis only. P6 is real.*

- Casey expects an admin view of what a pilot must prove.
- She sees the same clinician Home. The Insights page has numbers, but nothing ties them to pilot measures like care gaps or first adult visits (Shulman 2018, Spaic 2019).
- P6's warning: tools fall flat "when it gets into the day-to-day". She wants a frontline champion.

**Reached home: yes, but the home is built for a clinician, not for the person deciding whether to keep paying.**

## 7. Dr. Taylor, adult endocrinologist, first card she's ever seen

*Basis: Dr. Taylor (AI) is a hypothesis only. P3, C1 and C2 are real.*

**Screen 1. Consent screen.**
- I see: "Maya R. shared their Folio profile with your team. Maya chose what you can see." Then a list of what I'll see, "logged, closes after 10 minutes", and Open profile or Not now.
- I think: "What is Folio? Who runs it? Who wrote this?" C1 asked the same: "set up by, like, Maya? Or... all the doctors?"
- "Maya chose what you can see" overstates it. She set sharing switches weeks ago and didn't choose anything at this tap.
- The access log will record me only as "Adult clinic (ER or clinic tap)". Maya will never know which clinic looked.

**Screen 2. Profile (her home dashboard).**
- First screen: name, age, ID and type 1 diabetes, then a medication list. "How to work with Maya" starts just above the fold line.
- I'm looking for: allergies (not there anywhere), who takes over each part of care (it's in the handoff note, but that note is a draft so it's hidden), whether she has a family doctor (a row says "Not added yet" far below), and a call button for her emergency contact (two screens down).
- P3 wants "this is the medications" and "who's taking over". C2: "in mayas words should be at the top".
- A designer note, "Fold: below this line is context", shows to real doctors.

**Reached home: yes, in one tap. But the first screen answers "what meds" and not "who is she, what's risky, who do I call".**

## 8. ER doctor, one minute, Maya can't speak

*Basis: P1 (real, nurse who covers paediatric crises). Research on emergency summaries.*

- P1: "if they can't speak for themselves, you need this card to say something."
- One-step consent is right for the ER. No tour, no pop-ups.
- **Needs first:** allergies, insulin and devices, emergency contact, last confirmed date (Callahan 2023, PS-CA). Folio shows medications and devices first, but no allergies, and the contact is far down.

**Reached home: yes. One missing field (allergies) and one buried button (call her mom) are what an ER notices.**

## Patterns across all eight

1. **No setup, no identity.** Teens, parents and clinicians all land inside someone else's account.
2. **No "what is this" moment for newcomers.** The splash is a brand moment, not an explanation. The tagline alone doesn't say what Folio does.
3. **Deficit wording greets new teens.** "Family does everything. 0 of 5" is the first sentence a 13 year old reads.
4. **No single next step.** The plant explains what grows it, but there's nothing to tap.
5. **Labels that overstate.** "Shown to future care teams" on a hidden draft. "Maya chose what you can see." A card for a teen who has no card yet.
6. **The adult first screen misses allergies and who to call.**
7. **Parents have nowhere to go**, even though parent-to-teen handover is your strongest new theme.
8. **Role is invisible in the portal.** Everyone is Dr. Okafor.

## Does the research agree with how we think about users?

| What we assumed | What the research says | Strength | So for Folio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teens want privacy and control | Canadian paediatricians are told to see teens alone for part of each visit and not to ask about it in front of parents. Patient portals can't yet protect teen confidentiality (Agostino and Toulany 2023) | Guideline | Explain privacy on the teen's own phone, in plain words, before anything is shared |
| Parents should step back | Parents of 12 to 17 year olds worry most about being too involved. Live CGM sharing gave peace of mind but caused friction (Fremont and Miller 2021). Controlling talk was linked with defiance and higher A1C, autonomy-supporting talk with ownership (Goethals 2019) | Small study and cross-sectional | A parent role that is "back up, not run", with the teen holding the switches and skills shown, not numbers |
| Levels show readiness | No readiness tool has been shown to predict transition outcomes (Parfeniuk 2020). Teen and doctor ratings line up only weakly, r = 0.28 (Al Khalifah 2022) | Systematic review | Call levels a record of what the teen and team agreed, not a score. A teen's self-check is "your view", never a level change |
| Gamified rewards keep teens engaged | Toronto's gamified bant app saw use fade, with no A1C change (Goyal 2017). Paid glucose checks worked only while paid (Wong 2017). Broken streaks reduce later use (Silverman and Barasch 2023) | Trials | Keep the plant forgiving (never wilts, no streaks), and make "level only" a real choice on day one, not a hidden prototype switch |
| Rewards must never touch glucose | Expert consensus asks for neutral language about numbers (Dickinson 2017). Stigma affected 65.5% of Canadian youth and doubled the odds of poor control (Brazeau 2018) | Consensus and large survey | Already true in the code. Say it out loud in setup so teens trust it |
| Ask for notifications at the right time | Apple and Google both say never ask at launch. Ask when the benefit is obvious, behind one "Continue" button (Apple HIG, web.dev) | Industry guidance | No notification ask in setup. Folio already uses calendar reminders, which need no permission |
| Clinicians want a quick tour | A study of 70 users found card tutorials didn't help (91% vs 94% success) and made tasks feel harder (NN/g 2020). Training tied to real workflow is what predicts satisfaction (Longhurst 2019) | Industry and survey | No tour. One welcome card with real actions and a sample patient to practise on |
| Adult doctors need a summary | 11% of adult endocrinologists received a transfer summary, yet over 70% said it mattered (Garvey 2016). Canada's patient summary needs medications, allergies and problems (PS-CA) | Large survey and standard | An "At a glance" band with allergies, and a label on every item: confirmed by the team or written by the teen, with a date |
| Family doctors matter | In Ontario, 47% had a care gap over a year. Having no primary care visits was linked with higher DKA or death risk, HR 1.42 (Shulman 2018) | Large cohort | Show "no family doctor yet" as a prompt to the teen and a flag to the adult team |
| Folio should feel useful now | Coordinator benefits in a Canadian trial faded 12 months after support stopped (Spaic 2019). Young people valued health passports far less than professionals did (Colver 2019) | Trial and large program | Lead the welcome with something useful this week (learn a skill, book blood work), not "for when you're 18" |

**Where our assumptions don't hold up:**
- Folio starts at 13. P2 says 15 or 16, Diabetes Canada says plan from about 12, and Got Transition says 14 to 18. Starting at 13 is defensible, but the copy for a 13 year old has to be gentler.
- "Tap to open" assumes adult clinics will trust patient-held information. Clinicians judge it on accuracy, completeness and who wrote it (West 2017). So who wrote each part, and the date, must show on every item.
- Teens like points when asked (Jeminiwa 2019), but use fades. The plant should be tested against "level only", which is why the switch matters.

## The change plan

**Core** changes fix the first run for every persona. **Decide** changes need your call first (see the questions at the end). Everything stays on your computer until you've reviewed it.

### A. Patient app: a real first run (Core, large)

New files `docs/app/welcome.js` and `welcome.css`. They load after `app.js`, and the splash stays.

1. **Welcome.** Folio mark, then "Take over your diabetes care, one skill at a time." Below that: "Your clinic signs off each step with you. Folio keeps your story so new doctors don't start from zero." Buttons: **Set up Folio**, and if D is approved, **I'm a parent or caregiver**. A small link reads "Folio team: open the demo" and goes straight to Maya.
2. **Find your profile.** "Type the 8 numbers on your Folio card or your clinic letter." The input is digits only, shown as 4 and 4. The error says what to do next: "We couldn't find that number. Check the 8 numbers on your card, or ask your clinic." A labelled "Demo numbers" list is there for testing.
3. **Is this you?** Shows the avatar, name, age and clinic, then **Yes, that's me** or **Try another number**.
4. **Your space, your call.** Four short lines, each true in the code today:
   - Your clinic started your profile. You add the rest.
   - New doctors only see it when you tap or show your card, and you can see every time it's opened.
   - Folio never shows your glucose numbers and never stores health records.
   - Your parents only see what you turn on (if D is built). Otherwise: Folio has no parent access.

   It ends with "Change this any time in About me" and one **Got it** button.
5. **Make it yours.** "What should we call you?" (pre-filled, saved as the nickname). Then "How do you want to see progress?": **Plant**, **Stickers** or **Just my level**, each with a tiny picture and "You can switch any time." The Motivation style choice moves out of the hidden Prototype section into About me, under Display.
6. **What do you already do?** (Decide, E.) Five skills with I do it, With help or Not yet, plus "This goes to your care team as your view. It doesn't change your level." Skippable.
7. **You're set.** The seed or current plant. If Folio isn't on the home screen yet, it shows the Share, then Add to Home Screen steps. Then **Go to my Folio**, which opens Home.

- **No notification request anywhere in setup.**
- Setup is remembered per device. About me, Prototype gets "Show the welcome again". `?welcome=1` forces it for demos.
- Teammates who already used the app on a device skip it.

### B. Patient Home: first impression (Core, medium, in `app.js` and `data.js`)

- **One number.** The plant card heading stays the plant's name (Seed, Young plant). The line under it becomes "Level 4 of 10 · Contributor". "Stage 4 of 10" goes.
- **Plain, strength-first sentence.** Add a teen-voice line to each level in `data.js`, for example L1 "Your family leads for now. You're learning by watching." and L4 "Your family guides, and you pitch in." Show "3 of 5 skills on your own" only when it's above zero.
- **Your next step.** One card naming the first skill still at Learning, its guide link, and "I practised this". It replaces the anonymous "I practised a skill" button. The Working toward list shows the rest.
- **The note slot shows the newest note to the teen.** Drafts are never shown, and the label matches what's true: "Future care teams can see this too" or "Only you and your paediatric team see this."
- **No card yet.** "Your Folio card is on its way. Your clinic gives it to you at your next visit. Your Folio ID is 2276 7089." Show QR and Tap to share are hidden.

### C. Adult view: first open and first screen (Core, medium, in `card.js`, `card.css` and `data.js`)

- **Consent screen:**
  - Add one line on what Folio is: "Folio is a transition program from Sample Children's Hospital. It links to records and stores none."
  - Replace "Maya chose what you can see" with "Shared using Maya's sharing settings."
  - Add an optional "Your name and clinic" field. It names the viewer in Maya's "Who has looked at my profile" and pre-fills the clinician note form. Open profile stays one tap.
- **At a glance band** at the top of the profile:
  - Condition and devices (from the meds list)
  - Allergies (a new field the paediatric team confirms, like meds)
  - Emergency contact with a Call button
  - Who helps with big decisions
  - Adult care status: who takes over, and "No family doctor yet" when that's true
  - Maya's top note in her own words

  Every item carries "From Maya" or "Confirmed by Dr. Okafor, Oct 12, 2026".
- **New order:** At a glance, How to work with, Support in this visit, then the full medication list.
- **The fold label** shows only in demo mode.
- **Demo data:**
  - Maya's handoff note becomes shared and final, so who takes over shows.
  - A draft example moves to Jordan.
  - Maya gets an allergy: penicillin, rash (fictional).
  - "Find an adult family doctor" joins the Run my care tasks (k5).

### D. Parent first run and parent home (Decide, large)

- From Welcome, "I'm a parent or caregiver" opens three short screens:
  1. Your role moves from doing to backing up.
  2. Your teen decides what you see. Folio never shows glucose numbers or private notes.
  3. Enter your teen's Folio ID.
- If the teen hasn't turned sharing on, the parent sees "Eli hasn't turned on sharing yet. Ask them to open About me, Sharing and privacy."
- **Parent home:**
  - Level and skills, never notes
  - Who does what: the Run my care tasks, each marked Parent, Shared or teen, with "ready to hand over" suggestions
  - The next visit
  - Three "how to back them up" tips based on Goethals 2019
- **Teen side:**
  - A "My parent can see" switch group in About me (level and skills, care tasks, next visit). All off by default.
  - A visible "Your parent can see: ..." line whenever any switch is on (UK Children's Code: an obvious sign when monitored).
- **Data:** this adds `parentShare` and an `owner` on care tasks. It's a data shape change, so CONTRIBUTING asks us to tell the group first.

### E. Teen self-check in setup, shown in Visit mode (Decide, small to medium)

- The welcome step from A6 saves `selfCheck` with a date.
- Portal Visit mode shows "Eli's view: I do it" next to each skill. It never changes the level.

### F. Portal: sign in and a welcome that fits the visit (Core, medium, in `portal.js`, `home.js` and `store.js`)

- **Sign-in screen** (clearly marked "Prototype, no real sign-in"):
  - "Sign in with your Sample Children's Hospital account" leads to "Choose your account", the team list with each role and what it can do.
  - The choice sets who is signed in, and the Home greeting follows it.
  - The profile corner gets **Sign out**.
  - No password field.
- **One welcome card per person, first visit only.** No tour.
  - Line one, for everyone: "Folio records the readiness check you already do. It doesn't add a form."
  - Then role-specific lines:
    - Clinician and Admin: check skills in Visit mode, sign off a level in person, write the handoff once.
    - Nurse: "Nurses update skills and write notes. A clinician signs off levels."
    - Coordinator: "Add patients and hand out cards. Clinical notes stay hidden for your role."
  - Buttons: **Practise on a sample patient** (opens Visit mode with Maya) and **Got it**.
- **Coordinator Home:** "Cards to hand out" (patients with no card, like Eli) replaces "Ready to advance".
- **Admin Home:** "Average level" moves to Insights. Its place shows "Handoffs shared" and "Opened by adult clinics" (both counted from existing data).

### Not in this round (listed so we don't lose them)

- Showing that a "With help" status came from the teen's own "I practised" tap and not the clinic. Today the adult view can't tell the difference (West 2017).
- DKA and severe low history on the adult view. It's clinical data, and Folio's promise is to link records, not hold them. A team decision.
- Pilot measures on Insights (care gaps, days to first adult visit). The data doesn't exist yet.
- Real sign-in, a real date-of-birth check, and scannable QR codes.

### How I'll build it

1. Everything happens in `Folio-code` on `samantha/v2-research`, as **local changes only**. Nothing is committed or pushed until you've looked.
2. Same rules as before:
   - Brand tokens only, no new colours.
   - 44px targets.
   - Sentence case, no em-dashes.
   - The `h` safe template.
   - `data-action` handlers.
   - Store actions for every change.
   - `migrate()` fills in new fields, so saved demo data never breaks.
3. Bump `sw.js` to v9 so phones pick up the new files.
4. Update `CLAUDE.md` and the README "Why each feature exists" table with the new IDs, so you have rationale for the hi-fi screenshots.
5. **Test** by re-running all eight personas on a wiped browser, phone and desktop:
   - console errors
   - reduced motion
   - keyboard focus order
   - screen reader labels
   - the largest text size
