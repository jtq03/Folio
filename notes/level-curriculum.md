Each of the 10 levels has its own goal and 4 skills, 40 in all. A teen moves up when at least 3 of the 4 skills in their level are On my own (a program can set 2 to 4), a clinician signs off in person at a routine visit, and the teen confirms. The five areas (Know my health, Manage my care, Speak up, Run my care, Own my support) are kept as theme tags for colour and reporting.

Sources: Folio's earlier 10-level proposal (level names and "can" text), the TRAQ and READDY readiness domains (knowledge, self-management, speaking up, health system navigation, health behaviours) and the Canadian Paediatric Society 2023 transition position statement. No licensed questionnaire items are copied.

## Level 1: Observer. Learn your condition

*This is where it starts. You find out what type 1 diabetes is, why insulin matters and who is on your team, while your family leads.*

- **Phase:** Aware. **Who leads:** Family does everything. **Folio unlocks:** Parent-managed profile, Folio ID card
- **Plant:** Tiny sprout ("Pip"), curious, a bit sleepy, asks what everything is. Every plant starts as something tiny that does not know much yet. Pip only needs light, water and a little patience, just like you at the start.
- **Plant lines:** "Oh! Hi. I just poked out of the soil." / "Is this what sunlight feels like? Tell me more." / "I am small, but I am paying attention." Idle: "Pip is soaking up the light and listening."
- **Why this order:** Understanding comes first. A teen who can say what type 1 is and why insulin matters has a reason for everything that follows.

### L1-01 What type 1 is (Know my health)

**I can say in my own words what type 1 diabetes is, and that it is nobody's fault.**

How:
1. Read a short guide about type 1 with someone at home.
2. Ask your diabetes nurse to draw how insulin and sugar work.
3. Tell one friend or relative what type 1 is in two sentences.

- **What the clinic sees:** Explains type 1 in their own words at a visit.
- **Guide:** none yet
- **Sticker:** First page. circle, blue, book icon, matte, size M. The very first page of your story. A little dog-eared already, because good stories get read twice. *Matte vinyl circle, 40 mm, soft-touch finish.* Back: "Day one. Not your fault, and not the end of anything."

### L1-02 Why insulin (Know my health)

**I can explain what insulin does and why my body needs it every day.**

How:
1. Read Insulin at meals.
2. Look at your pens or pump with your care team and ask what each one is for.
3. Explain to a parent why you cannot skip insulin, even on a day you feel fine.

- **What the clinic sees:** Says what insulin does and why it is needed daily.
- **Guide:** Insulin at meals (r9, draft for educator review)

## Level 2: Learner. Know your daily routine

*Now you get to know the everyday stuff. You learn your insulins, how to check your glucose and what to do for a low, and you start answering for yourself.*

- **Phase:** Aware. **Who leads:** Family, with me present. **Folio unlocks:** Read-only view of my own profile
- **Plant:** Seedling ("Sid"), asks why about everything, likes a routine. A seedling has its first real leaves and a daily rhythm. Sid grows best with the same small things done every day.
- **Plant lines:** "Morning water, then sunlight. Same time tomorrow?" / "Why does that leaf go that way? Just wondering." / "I like knowing what comes next." Idle: "Sid is stretching toward the window, right on schedule."
- **Why this order:** Daily routines (insulin names, checking glucose, treating a low) are the safety floor, and answering for yourself starts the habit of speaking up early.

## Open questions for the diabetes educator

- Is the order right? In particular, should highs, lows and sick days (Level 5) come before food and activity (Level 4)?
- Are 4 skills per level and "3 of 4 on their own" the right bar, or should some safety skills (treating a low, sick days) be required at every level?
- Level 7 "Parties and relationships" covers alcohol, sex and safety. Is this the right level and wording for a 15 to 16 year old?
- Level 9 "Yes, no or not yet" and "My privacy rights" need a check against Ontario consent law (PHIPA, Health Care Consent Act).
- Which skills need a written guide? Skills with no guide yet: L1-01, L1-04, L2-02, L3-02, L3-03, L4-03, L5-04, L8-04, L9-01, L9-02, L9-04, L10-02, L10-04.
- Do the "What the clinic sees" lines match what a nurse educator can realistically observe in a routine visit?
- Should glucagon teaching sit with the low plan (L5-01) or earlier with treating a low (L2-03)?
- The readiness check samples 27 of 40 skills. Which unsampled skills most need a question?
